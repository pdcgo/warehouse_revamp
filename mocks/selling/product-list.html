Ready = units on a shelf you can sell today. Ongoing = units on restocks ordered but not yet accepted onto a rack. Both carry a count and a value, and the value is always at cost — a product has no catalogue price, so this screen can never show a selling figure. Ongoing value is an estimate (cost settles on what actually lands, #74), which is why it is labelled and never added to Ready. Cross-sell is another team ordering OUR product for its own shop: the row shows whether it is open to them, the markup they pay over our cost, and what they have actually moved in 30 days. The warehouse filter is not cosmetic — stock is held per warehouse, so choosing one restates every ready/ongoing figure and every tile as that warehouse's.

Open questions this mock raises, for the backend that follows: (1) Neither stock number exists on a product — both live in inventory_service, per warehouse, and today only warehouse roles may read them. A selling team's own list needs an owner-side stock read that does not exist yet. (2) Cross-sell has no model at all. ProductDiscover today exposes every team's catalogue to every selling team with no opt-in and no price — so "open / not shared", the markup, and the 30-day units are all NEW. Three decisions hide in there: is markup a percent or a flat per-pc amount (both are drawn above, deliberately), is it one number or per-team, and does it live on the product, on a cross-sell agreement, or in settlement_service where the money between teams already settles? (3) A product that is open to cross-sell but out of stock (row four) is a state somebody must own — do we auto-pause it, or let the other team discover the hard way? (4) Sorting by ready qty / value / cross-sold is a cross-service sort — the catalogue is paged by product_service and those numbers come from elsewhere, so it cannot be a plain ORDER BY. (5) Under a warehouse filter, a product with stock nowhere drops out of the list entirely — including the can't-sell rows you most want to see. Is "warehouse" a filter on rows, or only a lens on the numbers? (6) Archive vs Delete — the wire still says ProductDelete; only the UI word changes here, and Restore is a new RPC that can legitimately fail on a re-used SKU. (7) The Product column is the shared ProductListItem, and two of its props need a decision here: it renders the owning team as a badge, which on your OWN catalogue is the same name on all 128 rows (pass no teamId, or leave it and accept the noise), and it takes an optional stock badge that this screen deliberately does not use — ready stock is a column with a value beside it, and the same number twice in one row is how two figures start disagreeing.
